The aim of my work is to help cities make long-horizon planning decisions under climate uncertainty — when the data is incomplete, the future is non-stationary, and the institutions deciding are operating on timelines fundamentally misaligned with the systems they govern. In the next twenty years, every major metropolitan region will face climate impacts — coastal inundation, pluvial and fluvial flooding, extreme heat — whose magnitudes and frequencies sit outside the historical record their planning systems were built on. As these impacts intensify, it is critical to develop methods that let planners and policymakers act on imperfect physical-risk data without waiting for certainty that may never come. My work develops decision-grade methodologies for that translation — scenario architectures that operate inside formal planning timeframes, land-use simulations under deep uncertainty, and comparative frameworks that move between data-rich and data-poor governance regimes. I apply scenario modelling, robust decision-making, stochastic land-use simulation, and design-thinking methods to develop planning tools that decision-makers can actually use. I completed my PhD at TU Delft in 2026, where I developed methodologies for adaptive urban planning across the Metropolitan Region of Amsterdam and the Mumbai Metropolitan Region. Before that, I helped set up the Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure at UNDRR — a 39-nation multilateral mechanism launched at the 2019 UN Climate Action Summit — and I currently advise the Mumbai Metropolitan Region Development Authority on its long-term climate, infrastructure, and water-system strategy. I have published in npj Urban Sustainability, Cities, and Environment & Planning B, and serve as a First and Second Order Draft reviewer for the IPCC Special Report on Climate Change and Cities. I received the Allianz Climate Risk Research Award in 2024 and was named a C40 Women4Climate Fellow in 2022.
